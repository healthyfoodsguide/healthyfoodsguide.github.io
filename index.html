All 20 Healthy Food Guides

Superfoods

Superfoods is a marketing word, not a scientific one, but the foods that earn the label are genuinely nutrient-dense and worth eating regularly. A longer read with extra tips and serving ideas.

Leafy Greens

Leafy greens are the most nutrient-dense foods you can eat: low in calories, rich in folate, vitamin K, calcium and fibre.

Whole Grains

Whole grains keep the bran, germ and endosperm together, which means more fibre, more B vitamins and steadier energy than refined grains.

Healthy Fats

Healthy fats are essential: they help you absorb vitamins, protect your heart and keep you full, and the source of the fat matters more than the amount. A longer read with extra tips and serving ideas.

Best Fruits

Fruit is nature's fast food: fibre, water, vitamins and natural sweetness in one package, and eating whole fruit is very different from drinking juice.

Nuts & Seeds

Nuts and seeds pack healthy fats, protein, fibre and minerals into a small handful, which makes them filling but also easy to overeat.

Cruciferous Vegetables

Cruciferous vegetables such as broccoli, cauliflower and cabbage are linked with lower disease risk and are cheap, filling and versatile. A longer read with extra tips and serving ideas.

Berries

Berries are among the healthiest fruits: high in fibre and anthocyanins, the pigments that give them their colour, and low in calories.

Legumes

Legumes are the cheapest healthy food in the supermarket: beans, lentils and chickpeas are rich in fibre, protein, iron and folate.

Fermented Foods

Fermented foods such as yogurt, kimchi and kefir support gut health by adding live cultures and variety to the bacteria in your digestive system. A longer read with extra tips and serving ideas.

Herbs & Spices

Herbs and spices add flavour without calories, salt or sugar, and many of them, from turmeric to garlic, are studied for their health compounds.

Olive Oil

Extra virgin olive oil is the cornerstone fat of the Mediterranean diet, rich in monounsaturated fat and polyphenols that protect the heart.

Green Tea

Green tea is one of the most studied drinks in the world, rich in catechins and L-theanine, with a gentle lift and no calories. A longer read with extra tips and serving ideas.

Yogurt & Probiotics

Yogurt with live cultures is a practical probiotic food: protein, calcium and friendly bacteria in a single pot, as long as it is not loaded with sugar.

Seeds

Seeds are small but mighty: chia, flax, pumpkin and sunflower seeds add fibre, healthy fats and minerals to almost any meal.

Mushrooms

Mushrooms are low in calories and rich in B vitamins, selenium and umami flavour, and some varieties provide vitamin D when exposed to sunlight. A longer read with extra tips and serving ideas.

Sweet Potatoes

Sweet potatoes are a nutritional bargain: rich in beta-carotene, fibre and potassium, filling, and far more nutritious than white potatoes.

Eggs

Eggs are one of the most complete foods there is: high-quality protein, choline, vitamin D and B12, all for very few calories.

Salmon & Omega-3

Salmon is the best-known source of omega-3 fats EPA and DHA, which support heart and brain health, along with vitamin D and protein. A longer read with extra tips and serving ideas.

Dark Chocolate

Dark chocolate, in small amounts, is a genuine treat with benefits: flavanols, iron and magnesium, but only when the cocoa content is high.
